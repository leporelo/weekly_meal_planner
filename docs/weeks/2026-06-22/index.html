High-Protein Savory Lentil & Buckwheat Scramble with Spinach and Bell Peppers

🥜 38g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 25 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 100 g Red Lentils (dry)
  • 80 g Buckwheat Groats (dry)
  • 200 g Extra-Firm Tofu (pressed)
  • 4 tbsp Nutritional Yeast
  • 100 g Fresh Spinach
  • 2 whole Bell Peppers (any color)
  • 1 tsp Garlic Powder
  • 1 tsp Turmeric Powder
  • 0.5 tsp Black Salt (Kala Namak)
  • 2 tbsp Olive Oil
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black Pepper
Instructions
1. Rinse red lentils and buckwheat groats thoroughly. In separate saucepans, cook lentils with 300ml water until tender (about 15-20 min), and buckwheat with 200ml water until soft and water absorbed (about 15 min). Drain any excess water from lentils. 2. While lentils and buckwheat cook, press extra-firm tofu to remove excess water, then crumble it into a large mixing bowl. Add nutritional yeast, garlic powder, turmeric, black salt, regular salt, and black pepper. Mix well to coat the tofu and break it into small, 'scrambled' pieces. 3. Wash and dice bell peppers. Heat olive oil in a large non-stick skillet over medium heat. Add diced bell peppers and sauté for 5-7 minutes until slightly softened. 4. Add the seasoned crumbled tofu to the skillet and cook for 5-7 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the tofu begins to brown lightly and some moisture evaporates. 5. Stir in the cooked lentils, cooked buckwheat, and fresh spinach. Cook for another 2-3 minutes, until the spinach wilts and all ingredients are heated through. Taste and adjust seasoning if needed. Serve immediately.

High-Protein Spiced Chickpea & Quinoa Power Bowl with Roasted Zucchini and Tahini Drizzle

🥜 25g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 30 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 120 g Quinoa (dry)
  • 240 g Canned Chickpeas (drained & rinsed)
  • 2 whole Zucchini
  • 4 tbsp Tahini
  • 2 tbsp Lemon Juice
  • 1 tsp Smoked Paprika
  • 1 tsp Ground Cumin
  • 2 tbsp Olive Oil
  • 100 g Fresh Spinach or Mixed Greens
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black Pepper
  • 2 tbsp Water
Instructions
1. Preheat oven to 200°C (400°F). Rinse quinoa and cook according to package instructions (typically 1 part quinoa to 2 parts water) until all water is absorbed and quinoa is fluffy, about 15-20 minutes. 2. While quinoa cooks, dice the zucchini into bite-sized pieces. Toss zucchini with 1 tablespoon of olive oil, 0.5 tsp smoked paprika, 0.5 tsp cumin, salt, and pepper on a baking sheet. Roast for 20-25 minutes, flipping halfway, until tender and slightly caramelized. 3. Drain and rinse canned chickpeas. In a small bowl, toss chickpeas with the remaining 1 tbsp olive oil, 0.5 tsp smoked paprika, 0.5 tsp cumin, salt, and pepper. Add to the baking sheet with zucchini during the last 10-15 minutes of roasting, or roast separately for 15-20 minutes until slightly crispy. 4. Prepare the tahini drizzle: In a small bowl, whisk together tahini, lemon juice, and 2 tbsp water until smooth and pourable. Add more water if needed to reach desired consistency. 5. To assemble the bowls: Divide cooked quinoa between two bowls. Top with roasted zucchini and chickpeas. Add a generous handful of fresh spinach or mixed greens to each bowl. Drizzle generously with the tahini dressing. Serve warm.

High-Protein Seitan & White Bean Skillet with Spinach and Sun-Dried Tomatoes

🥜 50g protein/serving 🔪 10 min prep 🔥 20 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 300 g Seitan (store-bought, water-packed)
  • 240 g Canned White Beans (e.g., Cannellini, drained & rinsed)
  • 60 g Sun-Dried Tomatoes (oil-packed, drained)
  • 150 g Fresh Spinach
  • 150 ml Vegetable Broth
  • 1 tsp Garlic Powder
  • 1 tsp Dried Oregano
  • 2 tbsp Olive Oil
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black Pepper
Instructions
1. If using seitan in a block, slice or chop it into bite-sized pieces. If using strips, simply separate them. Drain and rinse the white beans. Roughly chop the drained sun-dried tomatoes. 2. Heat olive oil in a large skillet or frying pan over medium-high heat. Add the seitan pieces and cook for 5-7 minutes, stirring occasionally, until they are lightly browned and slightly crispy. 3. Reduce heat to medium. Add the chopped sun-dried tomatoes, garlic powder, and dried oregano to the skillet. Stir and cook for 1 minute until fragrant. 4. Pour in the vegetable broth and bring to a gentle simmer. Add the drained white beans, salt, and black pepper. Stir well and let it simmer for 3-5 minutes, allowing the flavors to meld and the sauce to thicken slightly. 5. Finally, stir in the fresh spinach. Cook for another 1-2 minutes, just until the spinach wilts. Taste and adjust seasonings as needed. Serve immediately as is, or with a side of your favorite protein-rich grain if desired.

High-Protein Garlic & Herb Baked Tofu Bites

🥜 26g protein/serving 🔪 10 min prep 🔥 30 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 300 g Extra-Firm Tofu (pressed)
  • 3 tbsp Nutritional Yeast
  • 2 tbsp Soy Sauce or Tamari
  • 1 tbsp Olive Oil
  • 1 tsp Garlic Powder
  • 1 tsp Dried Mixed Herbs (e.g., oregano, basil, thyme)
  • 0.2 tsp Black Pepper
Instructions
1. Preheat oven to 200°C (400°F). Ensure your extra-firm tofu is well-pressed to remove as much water as possible; this will help it get crispy. 2. Cut the pressed tofu into uniform bite-sized cubes, approximately 1.5-2 cm (0.5-0.75 inch) in size. 3. In a medium mixing bowl, combine the tofu cubes with nutritional yeast, soy sauce (or tamari), olive oil, garlic powder, dried mixed herbs, and black pepper. Gently toss until all the tofu cubes are evenly coated with the seasoning. 4. Spread the seasoned tofu cubes in a single layer on a baking sheet lined with parchment paper. Make sure not to overcrowd the pan for best crisping results. 5. Bake for 25-30 minutes, flipping the tofu halfway through, until the cubes are golden brown and firm. Allow them to cool slightly before serving. These bites are excellent for meal prep and can be stored in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 3-4 days.

High-Protein Savory Tofu & White Bean Hash with Roasted Bell Peppers & Spinach

🥜 28g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 30 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 300 g Extra-firm tofu
  • 1 cup Canned white beans (e.g., cannellini)
  • 1 whole Red bell pepper
  • 1 whole Green bell pepper
  • 90 g Spinach
  • 3 tbsp Nutritional yeast
  • 1 tsp Garlic powder
  • 1 tsp Smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp Dried thyme
  • 2 tbsp Olive oil
  • 0.5 tsp Black pepper
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
Instructions
1. Preheat oven to 200°C (400°F). 2. Press extra-firm tofu for at least 15 minutes to remove excess water. Then crumble it into small pieces resembling ground meat. 3. In a large mixing bowl, combine the crumbled tofu, rinsed and drained white beans, diced red and green bell peppers, nutritional yeast, garlic powder, smoked paprika, dried thyme, salt, and black pepper. Drizzle with 1 tablespoon of olive oil and mix well to ensure all ingredients are coated. 4. Spread the mixture evenly on a large baking sheet lined with parchment paper. Roast for 25-30 minutes, stirring halfway through, until the vegetables are tender-crisp and the tofu is slightly golden. 5. While the hash is roasting, heat the remaining 1 tablespoon of olive oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the packed spinach and sauté for 2-3 minutes until wilted. 6. Once the hash is roasted, remove it from the oven and gently fold in the wilted spinach. 7. Divide the hash into two equal servings and serve warm. This dish works well for meal prep; store in airtight containers in the refrigerator for up to 3-4 days.

High-Protein Lemon-Herb Tempeh & Couscous Power Bowl with Roasted Asparagus & Sun-Dried Tomatoes

🥜 32g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 20 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 200 g Block tempeh
  • 1 cup Whole wheat couscous
  • 1.5 cup Vegetable broth
  • 200 g Asparagus
  • 4 tbsp Sun-dried tomatoes (oil-packed)
  • 2 tbsp Fresh parsley
  • 1 tbsp Fresh dill
  • 1 whole Lemon
  • 2 tbsp Olive oil
  • 1 tsp Dried oregano
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black pepper
Instructions
1. Preheat oven to 200°C (400°F). Line a baking sheet with parchment paper. 2. In a medium bowl, combine the cubed tempeh with 1 tablespoon of olive oil, dried oregano, half of the lemon zest, salt, and black pepper. Toss to coat evenly. 3. Spread the seasoned tempeh cubes in a single layer on the prepared baking sheet. Add the trimmed asparagus pieces to the same baking sheet, tossing them with the remaining 1 tablespoon of olive oil. 4. Roast for 15-20 minutes, flipping the tempeh and asparagus halfway through, until the tempeh is golden brown and the asparagus is tender-crisp. 5. While tempeh and asparagus are roasting, bring the vegetable broth to a boil in a medium saucepan. Once boiling, remove from heat, stir in the whole wheat couscous, cover, and let sit for 5-7 minutes, or until all the liquid is absorbed and the couscous is fluffy. Fluff with a fork. 6. In a large bowl, combine the cooked couscous, roasted tempeh, roasted asparagus, chopped sun-dried tomatoes, fresh parsley, and fresh dill. Squeeze in the lemon juice and add the remaining lemon zest. Mix gently to combine. 7. Divide the power bowl into two equal servings. Serve warm or at room temperature. This meal preps well for lunch; store in airtight containers for up to 3 days in the refrigerator.

High-Protein Seitan & Adzuki Bean 'Meatballs' with Marinara and Roasted Zucchini

🥜 38g protein/serving 🔪 20 min prep 🔥 30 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 250 g Seitan
  • 1 cup Canned adzuki beans
  • 0.5 cup Breadcrumbs (vegan)
  • 3 tbsp Nutritional yeast
  • 1 tsp Dried oregano
  • 1 tsp Dried basil
  • 1 tsp Garlic powder
  • 0.5 tsp Smoked paprika
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black pepper
  • 2 tbsp Olive oil
  • 1.5 cup Marinara sauce (no onion)
  • 2 whole Zucchini
  • 2 tbsp Fresh parsley
Instructions
1. Preheat oven to 200°C (400°F). 2. In a food processor, combine the roughly chopped seitan, rinsed and drained adzuki beans, breadcrumbs, nutritional yeast, dried oregano, dried basil, garlic powder, smoked paprika, salt, and black pepper. Pulse until well combined and the mixture can be formed into balls (do not over-process into a paste). 3. Using your hands, form the mixture into approximately 12-14 'meatballs' (about 1.5 inches in diameter). 4. In a large oven-safe skillet or baking dish, spread the marinara sauce evenly. Arrange the seitan & adzuki bean 'meatballs' in the sauce. Place the sliced zucchini rounds around the 'meatballs'. Drizzle the zucchini with 1 tablespoon of olive oil and a pinch of salt and pepper. 5. Bake for 25-30 minutes, or until the 'meatballs' are heated through and slightly firm, and the zucchini is tender. 6. Remove from the oven, garnish with fresh chopped parsley, and serve hot. This dish is excellent for meal prepping; store leftovers in an airtight container for up to 4 days.

High-Protein Spiced Roasted Black Beans

🥜 22g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 30 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 400 g Canned black beans
  • 1 tbsp Olive oil
  • 1 tsp Chili powder
  • 1 tsp Cumin powder
  • 1 tsp Smoked paprika
  • 0.5 tsp Garlic powder
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Cayenne pepper
Instructions
1. Preheat oven to 200°C (400°F). 2. Thoroughly rinse and drain the canned black beans. This is a critical step for crispiness. Spread the beans on a clean kitchen towel or paper towels and pat them as dry as possible. Allow them to air dry for an additional 10-15 minutes if time permits. 3. In a medium bowl, combine the dried black beans with olive oil, chili powder, cumin powder, smoked paprika, garlic powder, salt, and optional cayenne pepper. Toss gently to ensure all beans are evenly coated. 4. Spread the seasoned black beans in a single layer on a baking sheet lined with parchment paper. Ensure they are not crowded, as this helps them crisp up. 5. Roast for 25-35 minutes, stirring every 10-15 minutes, until the beans are crispy and slightly firm. Keep a close eye on them to prevent burning, as oven temperatures can vary. 6. Remove from the oven and let them cool on the baking sheet for a few minutes; they will crisp up further as they cool. 7. Divide into two servings and enjoy as a crunchy, high-protein snack. Store any leftovers in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days, though they are best enjoyed fresh.

High-Protein Tofu & Black Bean Breakfast Burritos

🥜 37g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 20 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 340 g Extra-firm tofu
  • 240 g Canned black beans, rinsed and drained
  • 2 tbsp Nutritional yeast
  • 0.5 whole Red bell pepper, diced
  • 1 cup Fresh spinach
  • 1 tbsp Olive oil
  • 1 tsp Ground cumin
  • 1 tsp Smoked paprika
  • 0.5 tsp Garlic powder
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black pepper
  • 4 whole Large whole wheat tortillas
  • 0.2 cup Salsa (for serving)
  • 0.5 whole Avocado, sliced (for serving)
Instructions
1. Press the extra-firm tofu for at least 15 minutes to remove excess water. Crumble the pressed tofu into small pieces using your hands or a fork. 2. Heat olive oil in a large non-stick skillet over medium heat. Add the crumbled tofu and cook for 5-7 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it starts to lightly brown. 3. Add the diced red bell pepper, ground cumin, smoked paprika, garlic powder, salt, and black pepper to the skillet. Cook for another 3-4 minutes until the bell pepper softens slightly. 4. Stir in the rinsed and drained black beans, fresh spinach, and nutritional yeast. Cook until the spinach wilts, about 2-3 minutes. Remove from heat. 5. Warm the whole wheat tortillas. You can do this in a dry skillet for 30 seconds per side, microwave for 15-20 seconds, or wrap in foil and bake at 350°F (175°C) for 5 minutes. 6. To assemble each burrito, place a generous portion of the tofu and black bean scramble in the center of a warm tortilla. Fold in the sides, then roll up tightly from the bottom. Serve immediately with salsa and sliced avocado on the side.

David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 300 g Firm tofu, pressed and cubed
  • 2 cup Shelled edamame (frozen, thawed)
  • 200 g Whole wheat spaghetti or linguine
  • 6 tbsp Peanut butter
  • 4 tbsp Tamari or soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp Rice vinegar
  • 2 tbsp Maple syrup
  • 2 tbsp Lime juice
  • 1 tbsp Fresh ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp Minced garlic
  • 2 tbsp Sesame oil
  • 4 tbsp Water (for sauce)
  • 1 whole Red bell pepper, thinly sliced
  • 1 whole Large carrot, julienned
  • 0.5 cup Fresh cilantro, chopped
  • 2 tbsp Sesame seeds (for garnish)
  • 1 tbsp Olive oil (for cooking tofu)

While the pasta cooks, heat 1 tbsp olive oil in a large skillet or wok over medium-high heat. High-Protein Kidney Bean & Walnut Burger Bowls with Quinoa and Roasted Vegetables

🥜 37g protein/serving 🔪 25 min prep 🔥 30 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 400 g Canned kidney beans, rinsed and drained
  • 120 g Walnuts
  • 60 g Rolled oats
  • 2 tbsp Flaxseed meal
  • 4 tbsp Water (for flax egg)
  • 1 tbsp Tamari or soy sauce
  • 1 tsp Smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp Ground cumin
  • 0.5 tsp Garlic powder
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black pepper
  • 1 cup Dry quinoa
  • 2 cup Water or vegetable broth (for quinoa)
  • 1 whole Zucchini, diced
  • 1 whole Red bell pepper, diced
  • 2 tbsp Olive oil (for roasting vegetables and cooking burgers)
  • 4 cup Fresh spinach
  • 1 whole Avocado, sliced (for topping)
  • 0.2 cup Salsa (for topping)
Instructions
1. Preheat oven to 400°F (200°C). In a small bowl, combine flaxseed meal and 4 tbsp water; stir and set aside for 5 minutes to thicken (this is your flax egg). 2. Cook quinoa: Rinse the dry quinoa thoroughly under cold water. Combine 1 cup quinoa with 2 cups water or vegetable broth in a pot. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for 15 minutes, or until all liquid is absorbed. Let stand for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork. 3. Prepare vegetables: On a baking sheet, toss the diced zucchini and red bell pepper with 1 tbsp olive oil, salt, and pepper. Roast in the preheated oven for 15-20 minutes, or until tender-crisp and slightly browned. 4. Make burger patties: In a food processor, combine the rinsed kidney beans, walnuts, rolled oats, flax egg, tamari, smoked paprika, cumin, garlic powder, salt, and pepper. Pulse until well combined but still with some texture (do not over-process into a paste). Form the mixture into 2 large, thick patties. 5. Cook burgers: Heat the remaining 1 tbsp olive oil in a large non-stick skillet over medium heat. Carefully place the patties in the skillet and cook for 5-7 minutes per side, until golden brown and firm. Alternatively, you can bake them alongside the vegetables for 20-25 minutes, flipping halfway. 6. Assemble bowls: Divide the cooked quinoa among two large bowls. Top with a bed of fresh spinach, roasted vegetables, and one kidney bean and walnut burger patty. Garnish with sliced avocado and salsa.

High-Protein Savory Chickpea & Zucchini Pancakes with White Bean Cream

🥜 28g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 20 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 150 g Chickpea flour
  • 240 ml Water
  • 150 g Zucchini
  • 2 tbsp Nutritional Yeast
  • 1 tsp Baking Powder
  • 0.5 tsp Garlic Powder
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black Pepper
  • 1 tbsp Olive Oil
  • 180 g Canned Cannellini Beans (drained, rinsed)
  • 1 tbsp Lemon Juice
  • 1 tbsp Tahini
  • 2 tbsp Water (for cream)
  • 1 tsp Dried Dill
Instructions
First, prepare the zucchini pancakes. Shred the zucchini and squeeze out as much excess water as possible using a clean kitchen towel. In a large bowl, whisk together the chickpea flour, 240ml water, nutritional yeast, baking powder, garlic powder, salt, and black pepper until smooth. Fold in the shredded zucchini. Heat 1 tbsp of olive oil in a large non-stick skillet over medium heat. Pour about 1/2 cup of batter per pancake, cooking 2-3 pancakes at a time. Cook for 3-4 minutes per side, or until golden brown and cooked through. While the pancakes cook, prepare the white bean cream. In a small food processor or blender, combine the drained and rinsed cannellini beans, lemon juice, tahini, 2 tbsp water, dried dill, a pinch of salt, and pepper. Blend until completely smooth and creamy. If too thick, add an additional tablespoon of water. Serve the savory chickpea and zucchini pancakes immediately, topped generously with the white bean cream.

High-Protein Beluga Lentil & Farro Pilaf with Crispy Seitan and Roasted Asparagus

🥜 45g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 30 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 100 g Beluga Lentils (dry)
  • 100 g Farro (dry)
  • 720 ml Vegetable Broth
  • 1 whole Red Bell Pepper
  • 1 whole Carrot
  • 2 whole Garlic Cloves (minced)
  • 1 tbsp Olive Oil (for pilaf)
  • 1 tsp Smoked Paprika
  • 1 tsp Dried Thyme
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black Pepper
  • 200 g Seitan
  • 2 tbsp Soy Sauce
  • 1 tbsp Nutritional Yeast
  • 1 tsp Smoked Paprika (for seitan)
  • 1 tbsp Olive Oil (for seitan)
  • 1 whole Asparagus Bunch
  • 1 tbsp Olive Oil (for asparagus)
Instructions
Preheat your oven to 200°C (400°F). First, prepare the farro and lentil pilaf. Rinse the beluga lentils and farro thoroughly. Dice the red bell pepper and carrot, and mince the garlic cloves. In a large saucepan, heat 1 tbsp olive oil over medium heat. Add the bell pepper, carrot, and minced garlic, and sauté for 5 minutes until slightly softened. Stir in the smoked paprika, dried thyme, salt, and pepper. Add the rinsed farro and beluga lentils to the saucepan, then pour in the vegetable broth. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for 25-30 minutes, or until the farro and lentils are tender and most of the liquid has been absorbed. While the pilaf cooks, prepare the crispy seitan. Cut the seitan into bite-sized pieces. In a bowl, toss the seitan with soy sauce, nutritional yeast, smoked paprika, and 1 tbsp olive oil until well coated. Spread the seitan in a single layer on a baking sheet. Snap off the woody ends of the asparagus. Toss the asparagus with 1 tbsp olive oil, salt, and pepper, then spread them on a separate part of the baking sheet or a second sheet. Roast the seitan and asparagus for 15-20 minutes, flipping halfway, until the seitan is crispy and the asparagus is tender-crisp. Serve the warm farro and lentil pilaf topped with the crispy seitan and roasted asparagus.

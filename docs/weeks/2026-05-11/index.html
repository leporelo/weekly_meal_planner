High-Protein Seitan & Chickpea Hash with Spinach and Sweet Potato

🥜 39.5g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 20 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 200 g Seitan (pre-cooked)
  • 1 cup Chickpeas (canned, rinsed and drained)
  • 1 whole Sweet Potato
  • 4 cup Baby Spinach
  • 2 whole Garlic
  • 1 tsp Smoked Paprika
  • 0.5 tsp Cumin Powder
  • 0.2 tsp Turmeric Powder
  • 2 tbsp Olive Oil
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black Pepper
Instructions
1. Wash and dice the sweet potato into small, 1/2-inch cubes. Mince the garlic cloves. Dice the seitan into bite-sized pieces. Rinse the canned chickpeas thoroughly. 2. Heat 2 tablespoons of olive oil in a large non-stick skillet over medium-high heat. Add the diced sweet potato and cook for 8-10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until tender and lightly browned. 3. Add the minced garlic, smoked paprika, cumin powder, and turmeric powder to the skillet. Stir well and cook for another minute until fragrant. 4. Add the diced seitan and rinsed chickpeas to the pan. Cook for 5-7 minutes, stirring, until the seitan is heated through and the chickpeas are slightly crispy. 5. Stir in the baby spinach, salt, and black pepper. Cook for 2-3 minutes, or until the spinach has wilted. Divide the hash evenly into two serving bowls and serve hot.

Green Mountain Plant-Based 'Pork' Bao Bowl with Brown Rice and Bok Choy

🥜 29g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 15 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 200 g Green Mountain Plant-Based 'Pork'
  • 2 cup Cooked Brown Rice
  • 2 whole Bok Choy
  • 1 whole Red Bell Pepper
  • 2 whole Garlic
  • 1 tbsp Fresh Ginger
  • 3 tbsp Soy Sauce
  • 1 tbsp Rice Vinegar
  • 1 tsp Toasted Sesame Oil
  • 1 tsp Cornstarch
  • 2 tbsp Water
  • 1 tbsp Sesame Seeds
  • 0.5 tsp Chili Flakes (optional)
  • 1 tbsp Olive Oil
Instructions
1. Prepare your ingredients: Cook brown rice according to package instructions if not already cooked. Slice the Green Mountain Plant-Based 'Pork' into thin strips. Chop the bok choy into 1-inch pieces, separating the white stems from the green leaves. Core and slice the red bell pepper into thin strips. Mince the garlic and grate the ginger. In a small bowl, whisk together soy sauce, rice vinegar, sesame oil, cornstarch, and 2 tablespoons of water to create the sauce. 2. Heat 1 tablespoon of olive oil in a large skillet or wok over medium-high heat. Add the Green Mountain Plant-Based 'Pork' strips and cook for 3-4 minutes until lightly browned and heated through. Remove from the skillet and set aside. 3. Add the red bell pepper, bok choy stems, minced garlic, and grated ginger to the same skillet. Stir-fry for 3-4 minutes until the vegetables are slightly tender-crisp. Add the bok choy leaves and stir-fry for another 1-2 minutes until just wilted. 4. Return the cooked 'pork' strips to the skillet. Pour the prepared sauce over the ingredients, stirring constantly for 1-2 minutes until the sauce thickens and coats everything evenly. 5. Divide the cooked brown rice between two serving bowls. Top with the stir-fried 'pork' and vegetable mixture. Garnish with sesame seeds and chili flakes if desired. Serve immediately.

Spiced Red Lentil & Tempeh Coconut Curry with Sorghum

🥜 56g protein/serving 🔪 20 min prep 🔥 40 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 1 cup Red Lentils (dried)
  • 200 g Tempeh
  • 1 cup Sorghum (uncooked)
  • 400 ml Full-Fat Coconut Milk
  • 400 g Diced Tomatoes (canned)
  • 3 whole Garlic
  • 2 tbsp Fresh Ginger (grated)
  • 2 tbsp Curry Powder
  • 1 tsp Turmeric Powder
  • 1 tsp Cumin Powder
  • 1 tsp Coriander Powder
  • 0.5 tsp Cayenne Pepper (optional)
  • 2 cup Vegetable Broth
  • 0.2 cup Fresh Cilantro (chopped, for garnish)
  • 1 tbsp Lime Juice
  • 1 tbsp Coconut Oil
  • 0.8 tsp Salt
  • 0.5 tsp Black Pepper
Instructions
1. Prepare the sorghum: Rinse the sorghum thoroughly under cold water. Combine 1 cup of sorghum with 3 cups of water (or vegetable broth) in a saucepan. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for 45-60 minutes, or until tender. Drain any excess water and set aside. 2. While sorghum cooks, prepare the curry. Rinse the red lentils under cold water until the water runs clear. Crumble the tempeh into small, bite-sized pieces. Mince the garlic and grate the fresh ginger. 3. Heat 1 tablespoon of coconut oil in a large pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the minced garlic and grated ginger and cook for 1-2 minutes until fragrant. Add the curry powder, turmeric powder, cumin powder, coriander powder, and optional cayenne pepper. Stir continuously for 1 minute until the spices are toasted and aromatic. 4. Stir in the crumbled tempeh and rinsed red lentils. Add the diced tomatoes, full-fat coconut milk, and vegetable broth. Bring the mixture to a gentle boil, then reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for 20-25 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the lentils are tender and the curry has thickened. 5. Stir in the lime juice, salt, and black pepper. Taste and adjust seasoning as needed. Divide the cooked sorghum into two bowls and ladle the hot tempeh and red lentil coconut curry over the top. Garnish with fresh chopped cilantro before serving.

High-Protein Savory Tofu & Kale Bowl with Fonio

🥜 34g protein/serving 🔪 10 min prep 🔥 20 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 300 g Extra-firm tofu, pressed and crumbled
  • 160 g Fonio, dry
  • 400 ml Vegetable broth
  • 100 g Kale, chopped
  • 2 tbsp Nutritional yeast
  • 1 tbsp Tamari or soy sauce (gluten-free if needed)
  • 0.5 tsp Garlic powder
  • 0.2 tsp Turmeric powder
  • 0.5 tsp Smoked paprika
  • 3 tbsp Hemp seeds
  • 1 tbsp Olive oil
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black pepper
Instructions
1. Rinse the fonio thoroughly. In a medium saucepan, combine the fonio and vegetable broth. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for 10-15 minutes or until all liquid is absorbed and fonio is fluffy. Remove from heat and let it sit covered for 5 minutes. 2. While the fonio cooks, heat olive oil in a large non-stick skillet over medium heat. Add the crumbled tofu and cook for 5-7 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it starts to brown. 3. Stir in the nutritional yeast, tamari, garlic powder, turmeric, smoked paprika, salt, and black pepper. Cook for another 2-3 minutes, stirring continuously, until the tofu is well coated and fragrant. 4. Add the chopped kale to the skillet with the tofu. Cook for 3-5 minutes, stirring, until the kale has wilted to your desired tenderness. 5. To assemble, divide the cooked fonio evenly between two bowls. Top each with the savory tofu and kale mixture. Sprinkle hemp seeds over each bowl before serving warm.

Green Mountain Plant-Based 'Chicken' & Edamame Stir-fry with Brown Rice and Cashews

🥜 41g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 35 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 200 g Green Mountain Plant-Based 'Chicken' pieces
  • 150 g Brown rice, dry
  • 200 g Frozen shelled edamame
  • 1 whole Red bell pepper, thinly sliced
  • 100 g Carrots, julienned
  • 150 g Broccoli florets
  • 50 g Cashews, raw
  • 3 tbsp Low-sodium tamari or soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp Toasted sesame oil
  • 1 tbsp Rice vinegar
  • 1 tsp Maple syrup or agave nectar
  • 1 tsp Fresh ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp Cornstarch
  • 2 tbsp Water
  • 1 tbsp Cooking oil (e.g., canola or grapeseed)
Instructions
1. Cook the brown rice according to package instructions. Typically, combine 1 cup dry rice with 2 cups water, bring to a boil, then reduce heat, cover, and simmer for 40-45 minutes until tender. Fluff with a fork and set aside. 2. While rice cooks, prepare the stir-fry sauce: In a small bowl, whisk together tamari, sesame oil, rice vinegar, maple syrup, and grated ginger. In a separate tiny bowl, mix cornstarch with 2 tbsp water to create a slurry. 3. Heat cooking oil in a large wok or skillet over medium-high heat. Add the Green Mountain Plant-Based 'Chicken' pieces and stir-fry for 5-7 minutes until lightly browned and heated through. Remove from skillet and set aside. 4. Add red bell pepper, julienned carrots, and broccoli florets to the same skillet. Stir-fry for 5-7 minutes until vegetables are crisp-tender. Add the frozen shelled edamame and cashews, stirring for another 2-3 minutes. 5. Pour the prepared stir-fry sauce into the skillet with the vegetables. Bring to a gentle simmer, then stir in the cornstarch slurry. Cook, stirring constantly, for 1-2 minutes until the sauce thickens. 6. Return the cooked Green Mountain Plant-Based 'Chicken' to the skillet and toss to coat evenly with the sauce and vegetables. 7. Divide the cooked brown rice between two bowls and top generously with the stir-fry. Serve immediately.

High-Protein Chickpea & Spinach Kofta Curry with Quinoa

🥜 35g protein/serving 🔪 20 min prep 🔥 35 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 150 g Cooked quinoa, dry
  • 400 g Canned chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 150 g Fresh spinach
  • 60 g Chickpea flour (besan)
  • 1 tsp Garlic powder
  • 1 tsp Ground cumin
  • 1 tsp Ground coriander
  • 0.5 tsp Turmeric powder
  • 0.5 tsp Chili powder (or paprika for less heat)
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 1 tbsp Fresh ginger, grated
  • 400 g Diced tomatoes, canned
  • 250 ml Vegetable broth
  • 120 ml Full-fat canned coconut milk
  • 1 tsp Garam masala
  • 2 tbsp Olive oil
Instructions
1. Cook quinoa according to package directions (typically 1 part quinoa to 2 parts water/broth, simmer until water absorbed). Set aside. 2. For the kofta: In a large bowl, mash the drained chickpeas with a fork until mostly broken down but still a bit chunky. Add spinach, chickpea flour, garlic powder, cumin, coriander, turmeric, chili powder, and salt. Mix well until a thick dough forms. If it's too sticky, add a little more chickpea flour. 3. Divide the mixture into 8 equal portions and roll them into small balls. 4. Heat 1 tbsp olive oil in a large non-stick skillet over medium heat. Carefully place the kofta balls in the skillet and pan-fry for 5-7 minutes, turning occasionally, until golden brown on all sides. Remove from skillet and set aside. 5. For the gravy: In the same skillet, add the remaining 1 tbsp olive oil and grated ginger. Sauté for 1 minute until fragrant. Add diced tomatoes, vegetable broth, and coconut milk. Bring to a simmer, stirring well. 6. Stir in the garam masala and adjust seasoning if needed. Gently add the browned kofta balls back into the gravy. Reduce heat to low, cover, and let it simmer for 10-15 minutes, allowing the flavors to meld and kofta to absorb some gravy. 7. To serve, divide the cooked quinoa between two bowls. Spoon the chickpea and spinach kofta curry generously over the quinoa. Serve hot.

High-Protein Spiced Almonds & Toasted Hemp Seeds

🥜 25g protein/serving 🔪 5 min prep 🔥 15 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 150 g Whole raw almonds
  • 6 tbsp Hemp seeds
  • 1 tsp Smoked paprika
  • 0.5 tsp Cumin powder
  • 0.2 tsp Chili powder (optional)
  • 0.5 tsp Garlic powder
  • 0.2 tsp Salt
  • 1 tsp Olive oil
Instructions
1. Preheat oven to 160°C (325°F). Line a baking sheet with parchment paper. 2. In a medium bowl, toss the raw almonds with olive oil until lightly coated. 3. Add smoked paprika, cumin powder, chili powder (if using), garlic powder, and salt to the almonds. Toss well to ensure all almonds are evenly seasoned. 4. Spread the seasoned almonds in a single layer on the prepared baking sheet. Roast for 10-12 minutes, stirring halfway through, until fragrant and lightly toasted. Keep a close eye on them to prevent burning. 5. While the almonds are roasting, lightly toast the hemp seeds in a dry small pan over medium heat for 2-3 minutes, stirring constantly, until fragrant and slightly golden. Be careful as they can burn quickly. 6. Once the almonds are done, remove from the oven and let them cool completely on the baking sheet. This will make them extra crunchy. 7. Once cooled, combine the spiced almonds and toasted hemp seeds. Divide into two equal portions for snacking.

High-Protein Tofu & Spinach Breakfast Bowl with Millet

🥜 46g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 25 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 340 g Extra-firm tofu
  • 1 cup Dry millet
  • 2.5 cup Water (for millet)
  • 4 cup Fresh spinach
  • 4 tbsp Nutritional yeast
  • 2 tbsp Tamari (or soy sauce)
  • 1 tbsp Sesame oil
  • 1 tsp Garlic powder
  • 1 whole Red bell pepper
  • 0.5 tsp Salt (for millet)
  • 0.2 tsp Black pepper
Instructions
1. Rinse millet under cold water. Combine millet and 2.5 cups of water in a medium saucepan. Add 0.5 tsp salt. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for 15-20 minutes, or until all water is absorbed. Let stand covered for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork. 2. While millet cooks, press extra-firm tofu for at least 15-30 minutes to remove excess water. Cut the pressed tofu into 1cm cubes. Dice the red bell pepper. 3. Heat sesame oil in a large non-stick skillet over medium-high heat. Add tofu cubes and cook, stirring occasionally, until golden brown and crispy on all sides, about 8-10 minutes. Stir in the diced bell pepper and cook for another 3-4 minutes until slightly softened. 4. Add fresh spinach to the skillet with the tofu and bell pepper. Cook until spinach is wilted, about 2-3 minutes. Stir in tamari, garlic powder, nutritional yeast, and black pepper. 5. To assemble the bowls, divide the cooked millet between two serving bowls. Top each with half of the tofu and spinach mixture. Serve warm.

Spiced Black Lentil (Urad Dal) & Coconut Curry with Brown Rice

🥜 54g protein/serving 🔪 20 min prep 🔥 45 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 2 cup Whole black lentils (Urad Dal, dry)
  • 1 cup Brown Basmati Rice (dry)
  • 400 ml Full-fat coconut milk
  • 400 g Diced tomatoes (canned)
  • 2 tbsp Fresh ginger (grated)
  • 2 whole Garlic (minced)
  • 1 tsp Cumin powder
  • 1 tsp Coriander powder
  • 0.5 tsp Turmeric powder
  • 0.5 tsp Garam Masala
  • 0.5 tsp Red chili powder (adjust to taste)
  • 2 cup Vegetable broth
  • 0.2 cup Fresh cilantro (chopped, for garnish)
  • 1 tbsp Vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp Salt
Instructions
1. Rinse the whole black lentils (Urad Dal) thoroughly under cold running water until the water runs clear. In a separate pot, rinse the brown Basmati rice. Combine rice with 2 cups of water and a pinch of salt. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for 35-40 minutes, or until water is absorbed. Let stand covered for 5 minutes, then fluff. 2. In a large heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven, heat vegetable oil over medium heat. Add grated ginger and minced garlic. Sauté for 1 minute until fragrant, being careful not to burn the garlic. 3. Add cumin powder, coriander powder, turmeric powder, garam masala, and red chili powder to the pot. Stir continuously for 30 seconds to toast the spices, releasing their aroma. 4. Add the rinsed lentils, canned diced tomatoes, full-fat coconut milk, and vegetable broth to the pot. Stir well to combine. Bring the mixture to a boil, then reduce the heat to low, cover, and simmer for 30-40 minutes, or until the lentils are tender. Stir occasionally to prevent sticking. 5. Season the curry with salt to taste. Ladle the hot lentil curry into two serving bowls alongside the cooked brown rice. Garnish generously with fresh chopped cilantro before serving.

Spicy Roasted Broad Beans and Pepitas

🥜 31g protein/serving 🔪 5 min prep 🔥 20 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 1 cup Dried broad beans (fava beans)
  • 0.5 cup Pepitas (pumpkin seeds)
  • 1 tbsp Olive oil
  • 1 tsp Smoked paprika
  • 0.5 tsp Cumin powder
  • 0.5 tsp Chili powder
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
Instructions
1. Preheat oven to 190°C (375°F). Line a baking sheet with parchment paper. 2. If using dried broad beans, soak them in water for at least 4 hours or overnight. Drain thoroughly and pat very dry with a clean kitchen towel. (If using pre-cooked or canned broad beans, drain and rinse well, then pat dry). 3. In a medium bowl, combine the dried broad beans and pepitas. Drizzle with olive oil and toss to coat evenly. 4. In a small bowl, mix together smoked paprika, cumin powder, chili powder, and salt. Sprinkle this spice mixture over the broad beans and pepitas, tossing again until everything is well coated. 5. Spread the seasoned broad beans and pepitas in a single layer on the prepared baking sheet. Roast for 15-20 minutes, stirring halfway through, until crunchy and lightly browned. Keep a close eye on them to prevent burning. 6. Let cool completely on the baking sheet before serving, as they will crisp up further as they cool. Store any leftovers in an airtight container.

High-Protein Tofu and Black Bean Stir-fry with Soba Noodles

🥜 53.25g protein/serving 🔪 20 min prep 🔥 25 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 400 g Extra-Firm Tofu (pressed and cubed)
  • 240 g Canned Black Beans (drained and rinsed)
  • 150 g Soba Noodles
  • 300 g Bok Choy (chopped)
  • 150 g Bell Pepper (red or yellow, sliced)
  • 18 g Toasted Sesame Seeds
  • 1 tsp Garlic Powder
  • 0.5 tsp Ginger Powder
  • 45 ml Low Sodium Soy Sauce
  • 15 ml Rice Vinegar
  • 15 ml Sesame Oil
  • 5 ml Maple Syrup (optional)
  • 8 g Cornstarch
  • 30 ml Water (for slurry)
  • 15 ml Vegetable Oil
Instructions
1. Press the extra-firm tofu for at least 30 minutes to remove excess water, then cut it into 1-inch cubes. Rinse and drain the black beans. Chop the bok choy and slice the bell pepper. 2. In a small bowl, whisk together the soy sauce, rice vinegar, sesame oil, maple syrup (if using), garlic powder, and ginger powder to create the stir-fry sauce. In another small bowl, whisk the cornstarch with 2 tablespoons of water to create a smooth slurry. 3. Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Add the soba noodles and cook according to package directions, typically 4-6 minutes, until al dente. Drain the noodles and rinse them thoroughly with cold water to prevent sticking; set aside. 4. Heat the vegetable oil in a large wok or skillet over medium-high heat. Add the cubed tofu and cook, stirring occasionally, until it is golden brown and slightly crispy on all sides, about 8-10 minutes. Remove the tofu from the pan and set it aside. 5.

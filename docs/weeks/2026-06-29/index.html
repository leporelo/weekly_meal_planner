High-Protein Tofu, Chickpea & Spinach Scramble with Quinoa

🥜 27.5g protein/serving 🔪 10 min prep 🔥 25 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 400 g Extra-firm Tofu, pressed
  • 1 cup Cooked Chickpeas
  • 0.5 cup Uncooked Quinoa
  • 4 cup Fresh Spinach
  • 4 tbsp Nutritional Yeast
  • 0.5 tsp Turmeric Powder
  • 0.2 tsp Black Salt (Kala Namak)
  • 1 tsp Garlic Powder
  • 0.5 tsp Smoked Paprika
  • 2 tbsp Olive Oil
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black Pepper
Instructions
First, rinse the quinoa thoroughly under cold water. Combine quinoa with 1 cup (240ml) of water in a small saucepan and bring to a boil. Reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for 15 minutes, or until all water is absorbed. Let stand for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork. While quinoa cooks, crumble the pressed tofu into a large bowl. Add nutritional yeast, turmeric, black salt, garlic powder, smoked paprika, salt, and black pepper, mixing well to combine. Heat olive oil in a large non-stick skillet over medium heat. Add the seasoned tofu and cook for 8-10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it starts to lightly brown. Stir in the cooked chickpeas and fresh spinach. Cook for another 3-5 minutes, until the spinach is wilted. Divide the cooked quinoa into two bowls, then top with the high-protein tofu and chickpea scramble. Serve immediately.

High-Protein Smoky Tempeh & Black Bean Grain Bowl with Bell Peppers and Freekeh

🥜 43g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 30 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 200 g Tempeh
  • 1.5 cup Cooked Black Beans
  • 0.8 cup Uncooked Freekeh
  • 1 whole Red Bell Pepper, diced
  • 1 whole Green Bell Pepper, diced
  • 1 tsp Smoked Paprika
  • 1 tsp Cumin Powder
  • 0.5 tsp Chili Powder
  • 1 tsp Garlic Powder
  • 2 tbsp Tamari
  • 2 tbsp Lime Juice
  • 2 tbsp Olive Oil
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black Pepper
  • 0.2 cup Fresh Cilantro, chopped (optional)
Instructions
Rinse freekeh thoroughly. Combine freekeh with 1.5 cups (360ml) of water in a saucepan. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for 20-25 minutes until tender and water is absorbed. Fluff with a fork. While freekeh cooks, dice the tempeh into small cubes or crumble it. Heat 1 tablespoon of olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the tempeh and cook for 5-7 minutes until golden brown and slightly crispy. Remove tempeh from skillet and set aside. In the same skillet, add the remaining 1 tablespoon of olive oil. Add diced red and green bell peppers, smoked paprika, cumin powder, chili powder, garlic powder, salt, and black pepper. Sauté for 5-7 minutes until peppers are tender-crisp. Stir in the cooked black beans and the cooked tempeh. Add tamari and lime juice, stirring to coat all ingredients. Cook for another 2-3 minutes until heated through. To serve, divide the cooked freekeh into two bowls. Top generously with the smoky tempeh and black bean mixture. Garnish with fresh cilantro if desired.

High-Protein Creamy Tahini Pasta with Lentils and Roasted Broccoli

🥜 56g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 25 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 200 g Protein Pasta (e.g., chickpea or lentil pasta)
  • 1.5 cup Cooked Green Lentils
  • 1 whole Broccoli, cut into florets
  • 0.2 cup Tahini
  • 3 tbsp Lemon Juice
  • 1.5 tsp Garlic Powder
  • 3 tbsp Nutritional Yeast
  • 0.5 cup Water
  • 0.5 tsp Red Pepper Flakes (optional)
  • 3 tbsp Olive Oil
  • 1 tsp Salt
  • 0.5 tsp Black Pepper
Instructions
Preheat your oven to 400°F (200°C). Toss the broccoli florets with 1 tablespoon of olive oil, 0.5 teaspoon of salt, and 0.25 teaspoon of black pepper on a baking sheet. Roast for 15-20 minutes, or until tender and slightly charred. While the broccoli roasts, cook the protein pasta according to package directions in a large pot of salted water. Before draining, reserve about 0.5 cup (120ml) of the starchy pasta water. Drain the pasta and set aside. In a large bowl, whisk together tahini, lemon juice, garlic powder, nutritional yeast, water, 0.5 teaspoon of salt, 0.25 teaspoon of black pepper, and red pepper flakes (if using) until smooth and creamy. If the sauce is too thick, gradually add some of the reserved pasta water until it reaches your desired consistency. Add the cooked pasta and cooked green lentils to the bowl with the tahini sauce. Toss well to ensure everything is evenly coated. Stir in the roasted broccoli florets. Serve hot, dividing evenly into two bowls.

High-Protein Savory Polenta with Spiced Lentils and Roasted Tomatoes

🥜 30.5g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 25 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 200 g Quick-cooking polenta
  • 1000 ml Vegetable broth
  • 20 g Nutritional yeast
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black pepper
  • 300 g Cherry tomatoes
  • 2 tbsp Olive oil
  • 1 tsp Dried oregano
  • 300 g Cooked brown lentils
  • 100 g Baby spinach
  • 1 tbsp Apple cider vinegar
  • 2 tbsp Fresh parsley (for garnish, optional)
Instructions
Preheat your oven to 200°C (400°F). Halve the cherry tomatoes and place them on a baking sheet. Drizzle with 1 tablespoon of olive oil, sprinkle with dried oregano, salt, and black pepper. Roast for 15-20 minutes, or until softened and slightly caramelized. While the tomatoes roast, prepare the polenta. In a medium saucepan, bring 1000 ml of vegetable broth to a boil. Gradually whisk in the quick-cooking polenta, ensuring no lumps form. Reduce heat to low and continue stirring frequently for 5-7 minutes, or until the polenta is thick and creamy. Stir in the nutritional yeast, 1/2 teaspoon salt, and 1/4 teaspoon black pepper. Remove from heat and cover to keep warm. In a separate pan, heat the remaining 1 tablespoon of olive oil over medium heat. Add the cooked brown lentils and baby spinach. Sauté for 3-5 minutes, until the spinach wilts. Stir in the apple cider vinegar. Divide the savory polenta mixture evenly between two bowls. Top each serving with the spiced lentils and roasted tomatoes. Garnish with fresh parsley, if desired, and serve immediately.

High-Protein Lentil, Hemp Seed & Wild Rice Power Bowl with Roasted Root Vegetables

🥜 41.2g protein/serving 🔪 20 min prep 🔥 45 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 120 g Wild rice (dry)
  • 720 ml Vegetable broth or water
  • 200 g Carrots
  • 200 g Parsnips
  • 2 tbsp Olive oil
  • 1 tsp Smoked paprika
  • 0.5 tsp Ground cumin
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black pepper
  • 400 g Cooked green lentils (canned, rinsed, drained)
  • 100 g Mixed greens
  • 60 g Hemp seeds
  • 4 tbsp Tahini
  • 2 tbsp Lemon juice
  • 4 tbsp Water (for dressing)
Instructions
Rinse the wild rice thoroughly under cold water. In a medium saucepan, combine the wild rice with 720 ml vegetable broth or water. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for 40-45 minutes, or until the rice is tender and has absorbed the liquid. While the rice cooks, preheat your oven to 200°C (400°F). Peel and chop the carrots and parsnips into 1-inch pieces. Toss them on a baking sheet with 2 tablespoons of olive oil, smoked paprika, ground cumin, 1/2 teaspoon salt, and 1/4 teaspoon black pepper. Roast for 25-30 minutes, flipping halfway, until tender and lightly caramelized. For the tahini dressing, whisk together tahini, lemon juice, and 4 tablespoons of water in a small bowl until smooth and creamy. Add more water if needed to reach your desired consistency. To assemble the bowls, divide the cooked wild rice and mixed greens evenly between two large bowls. Top with the roasted root vegetables and cooked green lentils. Sprinkle generously with hemp seeds. Drizzle the tahini dressing over each bowl and serve.

High-Protein Seitan & White Bean Skillet with Rosemary & Artichoke Hearts

🥜 45g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 25 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 200 g Seitan (pre-cooked, unflavored)
  • 2 tbsp Olive oil
  • 510 g White beans (canned, e.g., cannellini), rinsed and drained
  • 200 g Artichoke hearts (canned, packed in water, drained, halved)
  • 400 g Diced tomatoes (canned)
  • 120 ml Vegetable broth
  • 1 tsp Dried rosemary
  • 0.5 tsp Dried thyme
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black pepper
  • 2 tbsp Fresh parsley, chopped (for garnish)
Instructions
Drain the seitan and pat it dry, then slice or cube it into bite-sized pieces. Heat 2 tablespoons of olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the seitan and cook for 5-7 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it's lightly browned and slightly crispy. Add the rinsed and drained white beans, halved artichoke hearts, canned diced tomatoes, and vegetable broth to the skillet. Stir in the dried rosemary, dried thyme, 1/2 teaspoon salt, and 1/4 teaspoon black pepper. Bring the mixture to a simmer, then reduce the heat to medium-low, cover, and let it cook for 10-15 minutes, allowing the flavors to meld and the sauce to thicken slightly. Remove the lid and stir well. Taste and adjust seasonings if needed. Divide the seitan and white bean skillet evenly between two plates or bowls. Garnish with fresh chopped parsley before serving. This dish pairs well with a side of crusty bread or a simple green salad.

High-Protein Savory Oat Porridge with Lentils, Spinach & Toasted Hemp Seeds

🥜 26g protein/serving 🔪 5 min prep 🔥 25 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 100 g Rolled Oats
  • 70 g Red Lentils
  • 700 ml Vegetable Broth
  • 100 g Fresh Spinach
  • 2 tbsp Nutritional Yeast
  • 1 tbsp Tamari (or soy sauce)
  • 0.5 tsp Smoked Paprika
  • 2 tbsp Hemp Seeds
  • 1 tsp Olive Oil
  • 0.2 tsp Black Pepper
Instructions
Rinse the red lentils thoroughly under cold water until the water runs clear. In a medium saucepan, combine the rolled oats, rinsed red lentils, vegetable broth, and smoked paprika. Bring the mixture to a boil over medium-high heat, then immediately reduce the heat to low, cover the saucepan, and let it simmer for 15-20 minutes. Stir occasionally to prevent sticking, ensuring the lentils and oats are tender and most of the liquid has been absorbed. If the porridge becomes too thick before the oats and lentils are cooked, add a splash more hot water or vegetable broth. Next, stir in the fresh spinach, nutritional yeast, and tamari. Continue to cook for an additional 2-3 minutes, or until the spinach has fully wilted into the porridge. While the porridge finishes cooking, heat a small dry skillet over medium heat. Add the hemp seeds to the skillet and toast them for 2-3 minutes, stirring frequently, until they are lightly golden and fragrant. Be careful not to burn them. Divide the savory oat porridge evenly into two serving bowls, drizzle each with a small amount of olive oil for richness, and then generously sprinkle with the freshly toasted hemp seeds and black pepper before serving warm.

High-Protein Seitan & Chickpea Mediterranean Bowl with Herbed Bulgur and Roasted Cauliflower

🥜 32g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 30 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 200 g Seitan (pre-made, plain)
  • 240 g Canned Chickpeas (drained and rinsed)
  • 80 g Medium Bulgur
  • 300 g Cauliflower Florets
  • 120 g Cherry Tomatoes
  • 0.5 whole Cucumber (diced)
  • 0.2 cup Fresh Parsley (chopped)
  • 2 tbsp Fresh Mint (chopped)
  • 2 tbsp Lemon Juice
  • 3 tbsp Olive Oil
  • 0.5 tsp Dried Oregano
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black Pepper
  • 160 ml Water (for bulgur)
Instructions
Preheat your oven to 200°C (400°F). In a large bowl, toss the cauliflower florets with 1 tablespoon of olive oil, dried oregano, salt, and black pepper until evenly coated. Spread the seasoned cauliflower in a single layer on a baking sheet. Roast in the preheated oven for 20-25 minutes, or until the cauliflower is tender and has lightly browned edges, flipping them halfway through the cooking time to ensure even roasting. While the cauliflower roasts, prepare the bulgur: combine bulgur with water in a small saucepan. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for 12-15 minutes until all the water is absorbed and the bulgur is fluffy. Remove from heat and let it sit for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork. Next, slice the seitan into bite-sized pieces. Heat 1 tablespoon of olive oil in a non-stick skillet over medium-high heat. Add the seitan pieces and cook for 5-7 minutes, stirring occasionally, until they are golden brown and slightly crispy. In a large mixing bowl, combine the cooked bulgur, roasted cauliflower, rinsed chickpeas, diced cucumber, halved cherry tomatoes, fresh chopped parsley, and fresh chopped mint. In a separate small bowl, whisk together the remaining 1 tablespoon of olive oil and lemon juice to create a simple, tangy dressing. Pour the dressing over the bowl ingredients and toss gently but thoroughly to ensure everything is well coated. Divide the herbed bulgur and vegetable mixture evenly between two serving bowls, then top each with the cooked seitan. Serve immediately for a fresh and hearty meal, or portion into airtight containers for convenient meal prep.

High-Protein Peanut Tofu & Edamame Curry with Brown Rice

🥜 38g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 45 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 340 g Extra-Firm Tofu (pressed, cubed)
  • 200 g Shelled Edamame (frozen)
  • 120 g Uncooked Brown Rice
  • 400 ml Full-Fat Coconut Milk
  • 4 tbsp Smooth Peanut Butter
  • 2 tbsp Red Curry Paste (ensure no onion)
  • 1 tbsp Fresh Ginger (grated)
  • 1 whole Red Bell Pepper (sliced)
  • 100 g Fresh Spinach
  • 1 tbsp Lime Juice
  • 1 tbsp Tamari (or soy sauce)
  • 300 ml Water (for rice)
  • 1 tbsp Olive Oil
Instructions
Begin by cooking the brown rice: Combine brown rice with water in a medium saucepan. Bring the mixture to a boil, then immediately reduce the heat to low, cover the saucepan tightly, and simmer for 35-40 minutes, or until all the water is absorbed and the rice is tender. Remove from heat and let it sit covered for 10 minutes, then fluff with a fork. While the rice cooks, press the extra-firm tofu for at least 15 minutes to remove excess water, then cut it into 1-inch cubes. Heat olive oil in a large skillet or wok over medium-high heat. Add the tofu cubes and cook, stirring occasionally, for 8-10 minutes until they are golden brown and slightly crispy on all sides. Remove the cooked tofu from the pan and set it aside. In the same pan, add the grated fresh ginger and red curry paste. Sauté for 1 minute, stirring constantly, until the spices are fragrant. Pour in the full-fat coconut milk, smooth peanut butter, and tamari. Stir well with a whisk until the peanut butter is completely dissolved and the sauce is smooth and well combined. Bring the curry sauce to a gentle simmer. Add the sliced red bell pepper and frozen shelled edamame to the simmering sauce. Continue to cook for 5-7 minutes, or until the vegetables are tender-crisp and the edamame is heated through. Finally, stir in the reserved cooked tofu cubes and the fresh spinach. Cook for another 2-3 minutes, just until the spinach wilts. Remove the pan from the heat and stir in the fresh lime juice. Divide the cooked brown rice evenly between two serving bowls, then generously ladle the hot peanut tofu and edamame curry over the rice. Serve immediately and enjoy the rich, aromatic flavors.

High-Protein Savory Roasted Pepitas & Sunflower Seeds

🥜 20g protein/serving 🔪 5 min prep 🔥 15 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 60 g Raw Pepitas (pumpkin seeds)
  • 60 g Raw Sunflower Seeds
  • 1 tbsp Tamari (or soy sauce)
  • 1 tbsp Nutritional Yeast
  • 0.5 tsp Smoked Paprika
Instructions
Preheat your oven to 150°C (300°F). In a medium-sized bowl, combine the raw pepitas and raw sunflower seeds. Add the tamari, nutritional yeast, and smoked paprika to the bowl. Toss all the ingredients thoroughly, using a spoon or your hands, to ensure that every seed is evenly coated with the savory seasonings. Spread the seasoned seeds in a single, even layer on a baking sheet lined with parchment paper. This helps prevent sticking and promotes even roasting. Bake in the preheated oven for 15-20 minutes, stirring the seeds halfway through the baking time to ensure they toast evenly and don't burn. Keep a close eye on them during the last few minutes, as seeds can go from toasted to burnt quickly. The seeds are ready when they are lightly golden, fragrant, and have a satisfying crunch. Remove the baking sheet from the oven and allow the roasted seeds to cool completely on the baking sheet; they will become even crispier as they cool down. Once cooled, divide the savory roasted seeds into two equal portions for a high-protein snack, or store them in an airtight container at room temperature for up to a week for easy grabbing.

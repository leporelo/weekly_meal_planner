High-Protein Tofu & Spinach Savory Oats

🥜 27g protein/serving 🔪 10 min prep 🔥 15 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 200 g Extra-firm tofu, pressed and crumbled
  • 100 g Rolled oats
  • 2 cup Vegetable broth
  • 3 tbsp Nutritional yeast
  • 100 g Fresh spinach
  • 1 tsp Garlic powder
  • 0.5 tsp Turmeric powder
  • 0.2 tsp Black pepper
  • 1 tbsp Low-sodium soy sauce or tamari
  • 2 tbsp Hemp seeds
  • 1 tbsp Olive oil
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
Instructions
Heat olive oil in a medium saucepan over medium heat. Add the crumbled tofu and cook for 5-7 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it starts to brown slightly. Stir in the rolled oats, vegetable broth, nutritional yeast, garlic powder, turmeric, black pepper, and salt. Bring the mixture to a simmer, then reduce heat to low, cover, and cook for 5-7 minutes, or until the oats have absorbed most of the liquid and are cooked through. Stir in the fresh spinach and cook until wilted, about 1-2 minutes. Remove from heat, stir in the low-sodium soy sauce or tamari, and divide into two bowls. Garnish each serving with hemp seeds before serving.

High-Protein Quinoa & Edamame Salad with Toasted Almonds and Lemon-Herb Vinaigrette

🥜 24g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 20 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 100 g Quinoa, dry
  • 200 g Shelled edamame, frozen
  • 1 whole Cucumber, diced
  • 150 g Cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 0.2 cup Fresh parsley, chopped
  • 30 g Slivered almonds, toasted
  • 2 tbsp Lemon juice, fresh
  • 2 tbsp Olive oil
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 0.5 tsp Dried dill
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black pepper
Instructions
Rinse the quinoa thoroughly under cold water. Combine quinoa with 200ml of water in a small saucepan. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for 15 minutes, or until all water is absorbed. Fluff with a fork and set aside to cool. Steam the frozen edamame according to package instructions until tender-crisp, usually 3-5 minutes, then rinse with cold water to stop cooking and drain well. In a large bowl, combine the cooked and cooled quinoa, steamed edamame, diced cucumber, halved cherry tomatoes, and chopped fresh parsley. In a small bowl, whisk together the lemon juice, olive oil, Dijon mustard, dried dill, salt, and black pepper to create the vinaigrette. Pour the dressing over the salad and toss gently to combine. Just before serving, stir in the toasted slivered almonds. This salad can be prepped ahead and stored in the refrigerator for up to 3 days.

Seitan and White Bean Stew with Herbs and Greens

🥜 43g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 35 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 200 g Seitan, cut into bite-sized chunks
  • 250 g Canned cannellini beans, drained and rinsed
  • 3 cup Vegetable broth
  • 1 whole Carrot, diced
  • 2 whole Celery stalks, diced
  • 1 whole Bell pepper (any color), diced
  • 2 whole Garlic, crushed
  • 400 g Canned diced tomatoes, undrained
  • 2 tbsp Tomato paste
  • 1 tsp Dried rosemary
  • 1 tsp Dried thyme
  • 1 whole Bay leaf
  • 100 g Kale, chopped
  • 1 tbsp Olive oil
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black pepper
Instructions
Heat olive oil in a large pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the diced carrots, celery, and bell pepper, and sauté for 5-7 minutes until slightly softened. Stir in the crushed garlic and cook for another minute until fragrant. Add the seitan chunks and cook for 3-5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until lightly browned. Stir in the diced tomatoes (undrained), tomato paste, vegetable broth, drained and rinsed cannellini beans, dried rosemary, dried thyme, bay leaf, salt, and black pepper. Bring the stew to a boil, then reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for 20-25 minutes, allowing the flavors to meld and vegetables to tenderize. Remove the bay leaf. Stir in the chopped kale and cook for an additional 3-5 minutes, or until the kale has wilted to your liking. Serve hot and divide into two bowls.

High-Protein Chickpea Flour & Silken Tofu Frittata with Spinach & Tomatoes

🥜 27.8g protein/serving 🔪 10 min prep 🔥 20 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 150 g Chickpea flour
  • 360 ml Water
  • 125 g Firm silken tofu
  • 20 g Nutritional yeast
  • 0.5 tsp Black salt (kala namak)
  • 0.2 tsp Turmeric powder
  • 1 tsp Garlic powder
  • 120 g Fresh spinach
  • 150 g Cherry tomatoes
  • 30 ml Olive oil
  • 1 tsp Salt
  • 0.5 tsp Black pepper
Instructions
1. In a blender or food processor, combine chickpea flour, water, firm silken tofu, nutritional yeast, black salt, turmeric powder, and garlic powder. Blend until completely smooth and lump-free. Set aside. 2. Halve the cherry tomatoes. Wash and roughly chop the fresh spinach. 3. Heat 1 tbsp (15ml) of olive oil in a large oven-safe non-stick skillet over medium heat. Add the spinach and cook until wilted, about 2-3 minutes. Add the halved cherry tomatoes and cook for another 2 minutes. Spread the vegetables evenly in the skillet. 4. Pour the chickpea-tofu batter over the vegetables in the skillet. Reduce heat to low, cover, and cook for 8-10 minutes, or until the edges are set and the top is mostly firm but still slightly wet. 5. Meanwhile, preheat your oven broiler. Once the frittata is mostly set, drizzle the remaining 1 tbsp (15ml) olive oil over the top and transfer the skillet to the broiler for 3-5 minutes, watching carefully, until the top is golden brown and fully set. 6. Remove from oven, let cool for a few minutes, then slice into two equal portions and serve immediately.

High-Protein Spiced Tofu & Lentil Power Bowl with Roasted Broccoli and Tahini Drizzle

🥜 47g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 30 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 350 g Firm tofu, pressed and cubed
  • 100 g Brown lentils, dry
  • 90 g Quinoa, dry
  • 300 g Broccoli florets
  • 60 ml Tahini
  • 30 ml Lemon juice
  • 60 ml Water (for dressing)
  • 5 ml Maple syrup (optional)
  • 5 g Smoked paprika
  • 5 g Cumin powder
  • 5 g Garlic powder
  • 2.5 g Turmeric powder
  • 45 ml Olive oil
  • 1 tsp Salt
  • 0.5 tsp Black pepper
  • 2 tbsp Fresh parsley, chopped
Instructions
1. Preheat oven to 200°C (400°F). Line a baking sheet with parchment paper. 2. Cook lentils: Rinse brown lentils thoroughly. In a medium saucepan, combine 100g dry lentils with 300ml water. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat, cover, and simmer for 20-25 minutes, or until tender. Drain any excess water. 3. Cook quinoa: Rinse quinoa thoroughly. In a small saucepan, combine 90g dry quinoa with 180ml water. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat, cover, and simmer for 15 minutes, or until all water is absorbed. Let stand for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork. 4. Prepare tofu and broccoli: Toss cubed tofu and broccoli florets with 2 tbsp (30ml) olive oil, smoked paprika, cumin powder, garlic powder, turmeric powder, and half of the salt and pepper. Spread evenly on the prepared baking sheet. 5. Roast: Roast for 25-30 minutes, flipping halfway, until tofu is golden and broccoli is tender-crisp. 6. Make tahini drizzle: In a small bowl, whisk together tahini, lemon juice, water, and maple syrup (if using) until smooth and creamy. Add more water if needed to reach desired consistency. Season with a pinch of salt. 7. Assemble bowls: Divide the cooked quinoa, cooked lentils, roasted tofu, and roasted broccoli between two bowls. Drizzle generously with tahini dressing and garnish with fresh chopped parsley. Serve warm.

High-Protein White Bean & Artichoke Heart Stuffed Bell Peppers with Quinoa

🥜 27g protein/serving 🔪 20 min prep 🔥 40 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 2 whole Bell peppers, large
  • 400 g Canned cannellini beans, rinsed and drained
  • 150 g Marinated artichoke hearts, drained and chopped
  • 90 g Quinoa, dry
  • 15 g Nutritional yeast
  • 1 tsp Dried oregano
  • 1 tsp Dried basil
  • 1 tsp Garlic powder
  • 60 ml Vegetable broth
  • 30 ml Olive oil
  • 1 tsp Salt
  • 0.5 tsp Black pepper
  • 2 tbsp Fresh parsley, chopped
Instructions
1. Preheat oven to 190°C (375°F). Lightly grease a baking dish. 2. Halve the bell peppers lengthwise and remove the seeds and membranes. Place them cut-side up in the baking dish. 3. Cook quinoa: In a small saucepan, combine 90g dry quinoa with 180ml water. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat, cover, and simmer for 15 minutes, or until all water is absorbed. Let stand for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork. 4. Prepare the filling: In a large bowl, mash the rinsed and drained cannellini beans with a fork, leaving some texture. Stir in the chopped artichoke hearts, cooked quinoa, nutritional yeast, dried oregano, dried basil, garlic powder, vegetable broth, 1 tbsp (15ml) olive oil, salt, and pepper until well combined. 5. Stuff the peppers: Spoon the filling generously into each bell pepper half. 6. Bake: Cover the baking dish with foil and bake for 30 minutes. Remove the foil and bake for an additional 10-15 minutes, or until the peppers are tender and the filling is heated through and lightly golden on top. 7. Garnish: Sprinkle with fresh chopped parsley before serving. Serve hot.

David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings

High-Protein Savory Mung Bean Scramble with Spinach & Cherry Tomatoes

🥜 30.3g protein/serving 🔪 10 min prep 🔥 30 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 150 g Dried Mung Beans
  • 4 tbsp Nutritional Yeast
  • 1 tsp Turmeric Powder
  • 0.5 tsp Garlic Powder
  • 0.5 tsp Black Salt (Kala Namak)
  • 100 g Fresh Spinach
  • 1 cup Cherry Tomatoes
  • 2 tbsp Olive Oil
  • 300 ml Water
  • 0.2 tsp Black Pepper
Instructions
Rinse the dried mung beans thoroughly. Place them in a medium saucepan with 300ml of water. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat, cover, and simmer for 20-25 minutes, or until the mung beans are tender but still hold their shape. Drain any excess water, if necessary. In a large non-stick skillet, heat the olive oil over medium heat. Add the cooked mung beans, nutritional yeast, turmeric powder, garlic powder, black salt, and black pepper. Sauté for 3-5 minutes, breaking up the mung beans gently with a spoon to create a scramble-like texture. Add the fresh spinach and halved cherry tomatoes to the skillet. Cook for another 3-4 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the spinach has wilted and the tomatoes are slightly softened. Taste and adjust seasonings if needed. Divide evenly into two bowls and serve warm.

High-Protein Roasted Chickpea & Kale Chips

🥜 23.5g protein/serving 🔪 10 min prep 🔥 35 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 400 g Canned Chickpeas, rinsed and drained
  • 150 g Fresh Kale, stems removed, torn into bite-sized pieces
  • 1.5 tbsp Olive Oil
  • 4 tbsp Nutritional Yeast
  • 1 tsp Garlic Powder
  • 1 tsp Smoked Paprika
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black Pepper
Instructions
Preheat your oven to 180°C (350°F). Make sure the rinsed chickpeas are very dry by patting them with a paper towel. In a large bowl, toss the chickpeas with 1 tablespoon of olive oil, 1 teaspoon of smoked paprika, 0.5 teaspoon of garlic powder, and a pinch of salt and pepper. Spread them in a single layer on a baking sheet and roast for 20 minutes. While the chickpeas roast, prepare the kale. In the same bowl, combine the torn kale pieces with the remaining 0.5 tablespoon of olive oil, 0.5 teaspoon of garlic powder, and a pinch of salt. Massage the kale for 1-2 minutes until it softens slightly and is evenly coated. After the chickpeas have roasted for 20 minutes, add the massaged kale to the baking sheet, spreading it out around the chickpeas. Sprinkle both the chickpeas and kale generously with nutritional yeast. Return to the oven and bake for another 10-15 minutes, or until the kale is crispy and the chickpeas are golden brown and crunchy. Keep a close eye on the kale to prevent burning. Let cool slightly before serving as a high-protein, savory snack.

High-Protein Breakfast Scramble with Lupini Beans & Tofu

🥜 55g protein/serving 🔪 15 min prep 🔥 15 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 350 g Extra-firm tofu, pressed
  • 240 g Canned lupini beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 whole Red bell pepper, diced
  • 100 g Fresh spinach
  • 4 tbsp Nutritional yeast
  • 0.5 tsp Turmeric powder
  • 0.5 tsp Black salt (kala namak)
  • 0.2 tsp Black pepper
  • 1 tbsp Olive oil
Instructions
1. Crumble the pressed tofu into a large bowl using your hands or a fork. Add nutritional yeast, turmeric powder, black salt, and black pepper. Mix well to combine, ensuring the tofu is evenly coated and seasoned. This gives it a 'eggy' flavor and color. 2. Heat olive oil in a large non-stick skillet over medium heat. Add the diced red bell pepper and sauté for 5-7 minutes until softened. Ensure the peppers are cooked through but still have a slight bite. 3. Add the seasoned crumbled tofu to the skillet with the bell pepper. Cook, stirring frequently, for 8-10 minutes, allowing the tofu to lightly brown and dry out slightly. This creates a better scrambled texture. 4. Stir in the drained and rinsed lupini beans and fresh spinach. Continue to cook for another 2-3 minutes, or until the spinach has wilted and the lupini beans are heated through. Serve immediately as a hearty, high-protein breakfast.

High-Protein Wild Rice & Mushroom Pilaf with Toasted Pecans and Baked Tofu

🥜 35g protein/serving 🔪 20 min prep 🔥 40 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 300 g Extra-firm tofu, pressed and cut into 1-inch cubes
  • 1 cup Dry wild rice
  • 3 cup Vegetable broth
  • 200 g Cremini mushrooms, sliced
  • 60 g Pecans, chopped
  • 1 tbsp Fresh thyme, chopped
  • 1 tsp Garlic powder
  • 0.5 tsp Smoked paprika
  • 2 tbsp Olive oil
  • 0 g Salt and black pepper to taste
Instructions
1. Preheat oven to 200°C (400°F). In a bowl, toss the tofu cubes with 1 tablespoon of olive oil, garlic powder, smoked paprika, salt, and pepper. Spread the seasoned tofu on a baking sheet and bake for 25-30 minutes, flipping halfway, until golden brown and slightly crispy. This ensures the tofu is firm and flavorful. 2. While the tofu bakes, cook the wild rice. Rinse the wild rice thoroughly under cold water. In a medium saucepan, combine the rinsed wild rice and vegetable broth. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for 40-45 minutes, or until the rice is tender and has absorbed most of the liquid. Fluff with a fork and set aside. 3. In a large skillet, heat the remaining 1 tablespoon of olive oil over medium heat. Add the sliced cremini mushrooms and sauté for 8-10 minutes, until they are golden brown and have released their moisture. Stir in the chopped fresh thyme during the last minute of cooking for aromatic flavor. 4. In a dry skillet, toast the chopped pecans over medium-low heat for 3-5 minutes, stirring frequently, until fragrant and lightly browned. Watch carefully to prevent burning. 5. Combine the cooked wild rice, sautéed mushrooms, and toasted pecans in a large serving bowl. Gently fold in the baked tofu cubes. Taste and adjust seasoning with salt and pepper as needed. Serve warm as a complete and comforting meal.

  • 1 tsp Garlic powder

Hearty Lentil & Quinoa Salad with Roasted Root Vegetables and Creamy Cashew Dressing

🥜 26.5g protein/serving 🔪 20 min prep 🔥 30 min cook
David: 2.0 servings Baska: 1.6 servings
Ingredients
  • 100 g Green or brown lentils, dry
  • 70 g Quinoa, dry
  • 2 whole Carrots
  • 2 whole Parsnips
  • 100 g Baby spinach or mixed greens
  • 70 g Raw cashews
  • 120 ml Water (for dressing)
  • 2 tbsp Lemon juice
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 tsp Maple syrup
  • 0.5 tsp Dried dill
  • 2 tbsp Olive oil
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black pepper
Instructions
Preheat oven to 200°C (400°F). Wash and peel the carrots and parsnips, then chop them into 2 cm bite-sized pieces. In a bowl, toss the chopped vegetables with 1 tbsp of olive oil, salt, and pepper. Spread them on a baking sheet and roast for 25-30 minutes, or until tender and lightly caramelized, stirring halfway through. Meanwhile, rinse the lentils and quinoa thoroughly. Cook the lentils according to package instructions (typically 1 part lentils to 3 parts water, simmer for 20-25 minutes). In a separate pot, cook the quinoa (1 part quinoa to 2 parts water, simmer for 15 minutes until water is absorbed). For the creamy cashew dressing, soak the raw cashews in hot water for at least 10 minutes, then drain. Combine the soaked cashews, 120ml water, lemon juice, Dijon mustard, maple syrup, dried dill, salt, and black pepper in a high-speed blender. Blend until completely smooth and creamy. Once the lentils, quinoa, and roasted vegetables are cooked, combine them in a large mixing bowl with the baby spinach or mixed greens. Drizzle generously with the cashew dressing and toss gently to combine. Serve warm or at room temperature.

  • 2 tbsp Fresh parsley, chopped
  • 1 tbsp Fresh dill, chopped

  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black pepper

Ingredients
  • 1.5 cup Cooked brown lentils

  • 0.5 cup Walnuts, chopped
  • 0.5 cup Rolled oats
  • 1 tbsp Flaxseed meal
  • 3 tbsp Water (for flax egg)
  • 3 tbsp Nutritional yeast
  • 1 tsp Smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp Dried sage
  • 1 tsp Dried thyme
  • 2 tbsp Soy sauce (or tamari)
  • 1 tsp Garlic powder
  • 2 tbsp Ketchup (for glaze)
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard (for glaze)
  • 3 cup Brussels sprouts, halved
  • 1 tbsp Olive oil (for sprouts)
  • 0.5 tsp Salt
  • 0.2 tsp Black pepper
  • 1 cup Vegetable broth (for gravy)
  • 1 tbsp Cornstarch (for gravy)
  • 1 tbsp Soy sauce (or tamari, for gravy)
  • 0.5 tsp Garlic powder (for gravy)
Instructions
Preheat oven to 375°F (190°C). Line a small loaf pan (approx. 8x4 inch) with parchment paper. In a small bowl, combine flaxseed meal and 3 tbsp water; let sit for 5 minutes to create a flax egg. In a large mixing bowl, mash the cooked brown lentils slightly with a fork or potato masher, leaving some texture. Add the crumbled seitan, chopped walnuts, rolled oats, flax egg, nutritional yeast, smoked paprika, dried sage, dried thyme, 2 tbsp soy sauce, and 1 tsp garlic powder. While the loaf bakes, prepare the gravy: In a small saucepan, whisk together vegetable broth, cornstarch, 1 tbsp soy sauce, and 0.5 tsp garlic powder. Bring to a simmer over medium heat, whisking constantly, until thickened. Remove loaf from oven and let rest for 5 minutes before slicing. Serve slices of meatloaf with roasted Brussels sprouts and a generous drizzle of gravy.
